摘要：Expansions of short nucleotide repeats cause diverse neurological disorders, yet how they perturb neuronal function remains unclear. Benign adult familial myoclonus epilepsy (BAFME) is caused by intronic pentanucleotide repeat expansions in genes including SAMD12. Here, we establish patient-derived cortical organoids and a microelectrode array platform, coupled with a frequency-decomposition-based analytical framework, to characterize BAFME-associated neuronal hyperexcitability and compare organoid electrophysiological features with those observed in patient electroencephalographic recordings. Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals altered expression of genes related to calcium regulation and α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptor function in BAFME cortical neurons. Consistent with these changes, BAFME organoids show an increased density of AMPA-receptor-positive synaptic puncta. Pharmacological AMPA receptor antagonism suppresses hyperexcitability. Antisense oligonucleotides targeting the expanded repeat RNA-which reduce pathogenic RNA foci without significantly affecting SAMD12 mRNA levels-also attenuate hyperexcitability. These findings indicate that targeting expanded repeat RNA mitigates aberrant cortical network activity in BAFME organoids.

摘要：BACKGROUND: Poststroke spasticity significantly impairs recovery, yet preventive strategies remain limited. This study evaluated the effectiveness of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) in reducing the development and severity of spasticity in patients with early subacute stroke, offering a possible early intervention. METHODS: In this double-blind, randomized, sham-controlled trial, 70 patients with early subacute stroke (7-30 days) were randomized to receive 3 sessions of low-frequency (1 Hz, 1000 pulses at 90% resting motor threshold) rTMS over contralesional primary motor cortex (n=35) or sham stimulation (n=35), alongside standard care. The primary outcome was upper-limb spasticity assessed using the modified Modified Ashworth Scale. Secondary outcomes included handgrip strength, muscle power assessed by the Medical Research Council scale, pain assessed by the Visual Analogue Scale, modified Rankin Scale, and Stroke Impact Scale 3.0. RESULTS: rTMS therapy significantly lowered spasticity scores at 1 and 3 months across all assessed upper-limb joints (P=0.001), improved handgrip strength (P=0.01), and prevented pain (P=0.001). Stroke Impact Scale scores at 1 month showed greater gains in activities of daily living, mobility, hand function, social participation, and recovery in the rTMS group (P=0.001). Functional independence (modified Rankin Scale) was significantly better in the rTMS group (P=0.006) at 3 months. On binary logistic regression, sham stimulation was associated with greater odds of developing spasticity at 1 month (shoulder odds ratio [OR], 8.29 [95% CI, 1.95-35.27], P=0.04; elbow OR, 7.62 [95% CI, 1.69-34.35], P=0.08; wrist OR, 5.18 [95% CI, 1.60-16.78], P=0.06). At 3 months, sham stimulation remained predictive, with higher odds of spasticity at the shoulder (OR, 15.3 [95% CI, 8.26-28.43]), elbow (OR, 13.43 [95% CI, 3.94-45.78]), wrist (OR, 19.9 [95% CI, 4.9-80.96]), and fingers (OR, 26.25 [95% CI, 5.19-132.87], all P=0.001). Minor adverse events like headache and light-headedness were observed in 5 patients. CONCLUSIONS: Low-frequency rTMS over contralesional primary motor cortex significantly prevented spasticity, improved motor recovery, reduced pain, improved functional outcomes and quality of life in early subacute stroke, supporting its role as a promising adjunctive therapy in early stroke rehabilitation. 摘要：Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) is a pleiotropic neuropeptide widely expressed in the central and peripheral nervous systems, with established roles in neurovascular regulation, synaptic transmission, and neuroinflammation. While CGRP has been extensively characterized in migraine pathophysiology, its role in epilepsy remains incompletely defined and is primarily supported by experimental evidence. Preclinical studies consistently demonstrate CGRP upregulation following prolonged seizures, particularly in models of status epilepticus, and suggest that CGRP signaling may contribute to key processes implicated in epileptogenesis. These include modulation of neuronal excitability, blood-brain barrier permeability, and neuroimmune responses, such as microglial activation. At the same time, CGRP has been shown to engage neuroprotective pathways, including anti-apoptotic and anti-oxidative mechanisms, as well as barrier-stabilizing effects, supporting a complex and context-dependent role. Shared pathophysiological features between migraine and epilepsy, such as cortical hyperexcitability, neuroinflammation, and cortical spreading depolarization, provide a conceptual framework in which CGRP may act as a convergent mechanistic mediator. However, direct evidence in human epilepsy remains limited, and the translational relevance of these findings requires careful interpretation. This review provides a focused synthesis of mechanistic insights from experimental models, highlighting the dual role of CGRP in seizure-related processes and delineating current gaps in knowledge. Potential clinical implications, including the effects of CGRP-targeting therapies, are discussed in a hypothesis-generating context, with emphasis on the need for rigorous studies to clarify their relevance in epilepsy. PLAIN LANGUAGE SUMMARY: Migraine and epilepsy sometimes occur together and share changes in brain activity. This review examines CGRP, a signaling molecule involved in migraine, and its possible role in seizures. Animal studies suggest that CGRP can either increase abnormal brain activity and inflammation or protect nerve cells, depending on the conditions. Early observations in people raise the possibility that migraine medicines blocking CGRP could also affect seizures, but they do not prove a benefit. Further research is needed to determine whether these medicines can safely reduce seizures or prevent epilepsy.

摘要：BACKGROUND: Technological integration offers opportunities to improve neuropsychological practice in epilepsy care. Compared to other healthcare fields, neuropsychology has been relatively slow to incorporate these advancements into clinical practice. OBJECTIVES: To describe the use of technology in epilepsy neuropsychology and identify priorities for technological development. METHODS: An online survey of multiple choice and open-ended questions was developed in English and translated into Spanish, then sent to practicing neuropsychologists working in epilepsy care across all six International League Against Epilepsy world regions. The survey collected data on the settings in which neuropsychological services are provided to people with epilepsy, the technologies currently in use, the barriers to technological adoption, and desired future developments. RESULTS: 179 neuropsychologists from 39 countries participated in the survey. The majority of respondents were women (69 %), aged 30-49 (60 %), and held a PhD (60 %). Key barriers to technological adoption included concerns around tool validation, financial limitations to purchase existing tools, and a lack of culturally and linguistically relevant normative data. Priorities for future development included predictive algorithms, automating psychometric aspects of clinical practice, and better leveraging of functional neuroimaging technologies. CONCLUSIONS: This international survey highlights that while epilepsy neuropsychologists are open to adopting new technologies in their clinical practice, there are several barriers hampering uptake across the field. Survey responses highlighted that technological developments that are practical, accessible, evidence-based, and suitable for culturally and linguistically diverse populations are required to meet the needs of the epilepsy neuropsychology workforce.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Functional/dissociative seizures (FDS) have limited treatment options despite the major societal and personal impact of this condition. The efficacy of psychological interventions varies considerably and is influenced by social, economic, and personal factors. To guide future research on the condition, a systematic review of the published literature on the psychological treatments available for FDS was conducted. Current psychological interventions including diagnosis communication, psychoeducation, psychotherapy, hypnosis, and behavioural therapies have yet to show sufficient evidence of long-lasting successful treatment for FDS. The frequency and impact of FDS requires continued treatment development beyond the limited options currently available. Such treatments have been systematically reviewed for this purpose. METHODS: A comprehensive literature search was carried out using the keywords: dissociative seizures, functional seizures, NEA, non-epileptic, pseudoseizures, psychogenic seizures, psychological treatment, hysterical seizures, and pseudoepileptic. Studies evaluating psychological treatments for FDS were included. Studies including patients with comorbid seizure disorders were also included. RESULTS: A total of 55 studies (58 publications) met the inclusion criteria. A wide variety of psychological interventions for FDS have been investigated. Existing evidence suggests that several psychological interventions may be effective, with cognitive behavioural therapy demonstrating the strongest evidence base. Metabolically, the MPTP-induced condition was associated with relative hypermetabolism involving especially audiovestibular cortical areas, accompanied by relative hypometabolism in frontal executive, paralimbic, and visual associative regions. Acute vestibular stimulation (ON vs. OFF) demonstrated state-dependent metabolic differences across post-implantation evaluations. During stimulation, increased metabolism predominantly involved vestibulo-thalamo-striatal, sensorimotor, and limbic-paralimbic networks. In contrast, OFF conditions showed relatively greater involvement in visual associative cortices and cerebellar vermis. CONCLUSION: Acute otolithic vestibular stimulation was associated with metabolic modulation of vestibulo-thalamo-striatal and sensorimotor-related networks in a parkinsonian non-human primate model. These findings provide proof-of-concept evidence supporting the capacity of vestibular neurostimulation to engage distributed central motor integration circuits.
