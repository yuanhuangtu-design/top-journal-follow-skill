摘要：BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Genetic testing is increasingly used in presurgical evaluation, but the yield of resection and ablation across germline genetic epilepsies remains uncertain. METHODS: We conducted a systematic review of MEDLINE (PubMed) and Scopus and added cases from 3 institutional cohorts and the Pediatric Epilepsy Research Consortium (PERC) databases, including individuals with pathogenic/likely pathogenic germline variants besides tuberous sclerosis who underwent resection or laser ablation. Etiologies were grouped into biologically informed categories (GATORopathies, vascular, overgrowth, copy number variants (CNVs), channelopathies, synaptopathies, and other). Primary outcome was seizure freedom (Engel I) at the last follow-up. Group comparisons used Fisher exact and Kruskal-Wallis tests (α = 0.05; Bonferroni when applicable). Prespecified sensitivity analyses stratified by lesional status, excluded GATORopathies, and restricted to literature-only cases. RESULTS: We included 224 literature cases (65 studies), 36 institutional cases, and 12 PERC cases (n = 272). The median follow-up was 24 months (interquartile range 12-48.0). Seizure freedom was achieved in vascular disorders 14/19 (73.6%), GATORopathies 79/120 (65.8%), CNVs 18/31 (58.0%), overgrowth 9/16 (56.6%), channelopathies 13/43 (30.0%), synaptopathies 4/18 (22.2%), and other 11/25 (44.0%) (overall p < 0.001). Among cases with available imaging results, 208/253 (82.2%) had epileptogenic MRI lesions, including 64% of channelopathies and 80% of the synaptopathies. In univariate contrasts (each category vs all others), odds of seizure freedom were higher for vascular disorders (2.64-fold, 95% CI 0.87-9.65) and GATORopathies (2.41-fold, 95% CI 1.44-4.09), and lower for synaptopathies (∼4.2-fold lower, OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.05-0.77) and channelopathies (∼4.5-fold lower, OR 0.22, 95% CI 0.09-0.48). Direction and magnitude were consistent across prespecified sensitivity analyses (lesional only, literature only, exclusion of GATORopathies). DISCUSSION: Resective surgeries can be effective in germline genetic epilepsies, but outcomes vary by pathway. Disorders with discrete, lesional substrates (GATORopathies, vascular) show the highest likelihood of seizure freedom, whereas channelopathies and synaptopathies, despite the presence of MRI lesions, have substantially lower yields even in lesional cases. Prospective, genotype-aware surgical registries with standardized reporting (EEG, imaging, and pathology) and time-to-event outcomes are needed to refine the selection of surgical candidates and quantify seizure and non-seizure-related outcomes.

摘要：IMPORTANCE: Despite the profound impact of neonatal and infantile-onset epilepsies on neurodevelopmental outcomes and mortality, consolidated global epidemiological evidence remains lacking. OBJECTIVES: To estimate the global incidence and prevalence of neonatal and infantile-onset epilepsies and epilepsy syndromes and to characterize their associated etiological profiles. DATA SOURCES: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus were searched from inception through December 18, 2025. STUDY SELECTION: Population-based observational studies reporting the incidence or prevalence of neonatal and infantile-onset epilepsies or epilepsy syndromes were eligible for inclusion. DATA EXTRACTION AND SYNTHESIS: Two authors independently and in duplicate performed study selection, data extraction, and quality assessment of included studies using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal checklist for prevalence studies. Generalized linear mixed-model random-effects meta-analysis was used to pool the estimates of incidence, prevalence, and etiology. Subgroup meta-analyses were conducted by country, World Health Organization (WHO) region, World Bank income, and diagnostic criteria. MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Cumulative incidence and prevalence of neonatal and infantile-onset epilepsies and epilepsy syndromes. RESULTS: A total of 86 population-based studies from 30 countries across 4 WHO regions were included. The pooled cumulative incidence of infantile-onset epilepsies within the first 2 years of life was 236.0 cases (95% CI, 164.7-338.2; 95% prediction interval [PI], 53.0-1044.3) per 100 000 live births. Infantile epileptic spasms syndrome (IESS) was the most incident DEE, with 31.6 cases (95% CI, 27.5-36.2; 95% PI, 13.7-72.6) per 100 000 live births. Among self-limited epilepsies, self-limited infantile epilepsy had the highest incidence at 13.0 cases (95% CI, 9.2-18.2; 95% PI, 6.4-26.4) per 100 000 live births. Subgroup analyses revealed limited geographical representativeness, with the majority of studies from high-income countries in the European Region. CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: The findings underscore a significant global burden of neonatal and infantile-onset epilepsies, primarily driven by DEEs, specifically IESS. Given substantial between-study heterogeneity, the pooled estimates should be interpreted as broad indicators rather than precise, generalizable rates. Geographic disparities, with a lack of data from the African and Eastern Mediterranean Regions, underscore the need for population-based studies in underrepresented regions and longitudinal research on survival, remission, and functional outcomes.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Evidence for the utility of sleep electroencephalography (EEG) in the diagnosis of epilepsy is inconsistent, often necessitating repeat visits and sleep deprivation (SD), which increases patient and resource burden. In our setting in India, patients are often referred for EEG immediately following consultation (single visit) and EEG sessions are extended to allow for spontaneous sleep acquisition without prior SD. We aimed to evaluate the success rate of achieving sleep during these extended outpatient recordings and its incremental diagnostic yield for interictal epileptiform discharges (IEDs). METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed 1762 outpatient EEGs from patients (45% female) with suspected or known epilepsy. All patients underwent an extended routine EEG (~40-50 min), incorporating standard activation procedures and aiming to capture spontaneous sleep where possible. Successful sleep acquisition was defined as the attainment of at least NREM stage N2. IED detection rates were compared between wakefulness and sleep across different epilepsy types. RESULTS: Sleep was achieved in 70.5% of patients (median age, 28 years; IQR: 16-55; range, 0.3-90). Sleep recording increased IED detection over wakefulness alone, particularly in children, in adults with IGE, and in self-limited epilepsies. Whether this approach can substitute for a sleep-deprived EEG requires prospective, head-to-head evaluation.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Seizure clusters, or acute repetitive seizures, are a phenomenon often experienced by patients with epilepsy and pose a significant treatment challenge for clinicians. Several rescue medications have been approved by the Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of seizure clusters - including diazepam rectal gel, intranasal midazolam, and intranasal diazepam - which are commonly prescribed for prolonged seizures or status epilepticus (SE). Conflicting evidence exists relating to the impact of seizure cluster treatment on hospital care utilization and health-related quality of life (HRQoL). We hypothesized that children prescribed rescue medication specifically including an indication for seizure clusters would have fewer seizure-related Emergency Department (ED) visits compared to those prescribed rescue medications only for SE. METHODS: This study was a cross-sectional cohort study from a single pediatric center. A total of 3216 children with epilepsy and rescue medications were included. Measured characteristics included sociodemographic factors, epilepsy features, HRQoL, and healthcare utilization. We modeled the probability of having ED visits for seizures in the 12 months following prescriptions of rescue medication for either SE only or both SE and seizure clusters. RESULTS: There was no difference in ED utilization between patients receiving a rescue prescription for SE only (n = 48, 3.1%) or both SE and seizure clusters (n = 64, 3.8%) (OR 1.12; 95% CI 0.74-1.71; p = 0.6). A multivariable model was created to evaluate factors associated with ED utilization. The acute social needs score was the only measured variable associated with increased odds of having a subsequent ED visit (OR 1.37; 95% CI 1.22-1.53; p < 0.001). SIGNIFICANCE: There was no difference in ED utilization between patients receiving a rescue medication prescription including seizure clusters and those only for SE. The social needs score was the only measured variable in this study associated with increased ED visits. These results highlight that prescribing rescue medications for cluster seizures may have less impact on healthcare utilization than acute social needs.
