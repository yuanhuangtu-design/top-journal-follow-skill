摘要：A previously healthy 5-year-old girl presented with several days of fever/malaise followed by acute neurologic decline. Head CT demonstrated fulminant right cerebellitis with edema and fourth-ventricle effacement concerning for impending herniation. She underwent emergent posterior fossa decompression. CSF showed mild pleocytosis, and MRI revealed right cerebellar nonenhancing T2/fluid-attenuated inversion recovery hyperintense lesions with evolving supratentorial cortical involvement. Despite empiric antimicrobials and initial immunotherapy (high-dose IV methylprednisolone and tocilizumab followed by IV immunoglobulin/plasma exchange), she developed progressive neuroinflammation with new seizures and markedly elevated CSF IL-6, prompting escalation to additional IL-6-directed therapy with tocilizumab and rescue intrathecal dexamethasone. She subsequently stabilized with radiographic improvement and meaningful neurologic recovery. Expanded antibody testing later confirmed anti-GABAA receptor (anti-GABAAR) encephalitis. This case highlights fulminant cerebellitis as a life-threatening presentation of anti-GABAAR encephalitis and underscores the importance of early posterior fossa decompression, parallel diagnostic evaluation (including expanded antibody panels), and coordinated, time-sensitive immunotherapy escalation in select critically ill presentations.

摘要：IMPORTANCE: Despite more than 30 approved antiseizure medications, many individuals with focal epilepsy continue to experience seizures, indicating a persistent need for novel therapies. OBJECTIVE: To determine efficacy and safety of the novel, potent KV7 channel opener azetukalner for treatment of focal seizures. DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This phase 3, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial of azetukalner was conducted over a 12-week double-blind period (November 18, 2022, to February 3, 2026) at 138 sites in North America, South America, Europe, and Asia Pacific. Adults with focal epilepsy experiencing 4 or more focal seizures per month despite stable treatment with 1 to 3 antiseizure medications were enrolled. Data were analyzed using a dataset derived from the database lock dated February 26, 2026. INTERVENTIONS: Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to receive placebo or 15 or 25 mg of azetukalner once daily with the evening meal for 12 weeks with no titration period. Participants completing the double-blind period could enter an open-label extension. MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary efficacy end point was median percentage change from baseline in monthly (28-day) focal seizure frequency during the double-blind period for azetukalner vs placebo. Safety and tolerability were assessed by recording treatment-emergent adverse events, changes in clinical laboratory findings, vital signs, and electrocardiogram. RESULTS: Of 607 adults screened, 374 participants (190 [50.8%] female; mean [SD] age, 40.0 [12.9] years) were enrolled, randomized and treated with placebo (125 participants), 15-mg azetukalner (125 participants), or 25-mg azetukalner (124 participants). Participants had a baseline median (IQR) of 12.75 (7.56 to 32.97) seizures per month and had previously tried and stopped a median (IQR) of 5 (3 to 8) antiseizure medications. Most (336 participants [89.8%]) were receiving 2 to 3 concomitant antiseizure medications. The median (IQR) change from baseline in monthly seizure frequency was -10.4% (-39.1% to 8.3%) for the placebo group, -34.5% (-61.6% to -12.3%) for the 15-mg azetukalner group (P < .001 vs placebo), and -53.2% (-77.2% to -28.8%) for the 25-mg azetukalner group (P < .001 vs placebo). Azetukalner was generally well tolerated, and treatment-emergent adverse events were comparable to those observed in the phase 2b X-TOLE trial. CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: This randomized clinical trial found that azetukalner demonstrated robust, rapid, and durable reductions in seizure frequency with a favorable benefit-risk profile. Treatment with azetukalner resulted in one of the largest median percentage reductions in seizure frequency vs placebo reported to date in patients with drug-resistant epilepsy, supporting azetukalner as a promising novel antiseizure medication with significant opportunity to improve clinical outcomes. TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05614063.

Side-to-side and other complex head movements as ictal signs in epilepsy: A video-EEG case series and review of literature.推荐

摘要：Side-to-side head shaking is often considered a distinctive clinical sign that differentiates functional seizures from epileptic seizures; however, it may also occur as an ictal phenomenon in focal epilepsy. We retrospectively reviewed all video-telemetry reports of patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy undergoing presurgical evaluation between 2015 and 2025. All reports were electronically screened for mentioning side-to-side or other complex head movements and the video-EEG of these patients was reviewed and included if associated with ictal EEG changes. Seventeen patients were identified who experienced 631 seizures in total, 201 of which included such movements (median: 2 per patient, range: 1-160). One patient (No. 5) accounted for 79% of these events and represented an outlier. Head movements were predominantly horizontal (93.5%), followed by mixed (5%) and rotatory (1.5%). While small-amplitude movements predominated overall, larger amplitudes were more frequent after excluding the outlier case. In six patients, head movements were the initial observed feature of seizure semiology and typically represented a brief component of the seizure, although prolonged in the outlier case. Interrater agreement ranged from fair to good across semiological variables. Presumed epileptogenic zones were heterogenous, including frontal, temporal, anterior quadrant, and hemispheric regions. Ictal side-to-side and other complex head movements can occur in focal epilepsies but lack localizing or lateralizing value. They likely reflect seizure propagation and involvement of broader neuronal circuits. Further studies are needed to clarify their mechanisms and clinical significance.
