摘要：Older adults with epilepsy (OAE) represent one of the fastest growing yet least studied subgroups within the epilepsy population, encompassing both individuals with chronic epilepsy persisting into later life and those diagnosed with late onset epilepsy after ages 50-60 years. This population faces a convergence of risk; epilepsy is associated with substantial cognitive comorbidity, with up to 80% of OAE demonstrating cognitive impairment and more than half meeting criteria for mild cognitive impairment (MCI), alongside 1.8-4-fold increased odds of developing dementia. Cognitive dysfunction in OAE is typically multidomain, affecting memory, executive function, and language, and has been associated with demographic, lifestyle, epilepsy, medication, comorbidity, genetic, and neuroimaging factors. Despite this convergence of risk, we identified no adequately tested intervention specifically developed for OAE. This narrative review synthesizes evidence from two historically parallel literatures, epilepsy self-management/rehabilitation programs and cognitive interventions developed for individuals with MCI, to identify strategies that could be merged or adapted for OAE. Epilepsy-focused programs, including foundational self-management models and cognitive interventions, have shown improvements in self-efficacy, quality of life, and select cognitive outcomes, but were evaluated largely in younger and middle-aged adults and have not been developed for or tested in OAE. Similarly, MCI-focused compensatory, restorative, and multimodal interventions offer adaptable strategies for preserving cognitive function but do not account for the complexity of epilepsy management, including antiseizure medication effects, polypharmacy, and seizure-related cognitive fluctuations.
