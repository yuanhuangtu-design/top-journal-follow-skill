摘要：BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: The thalamus is an emerging neuromodulation target in drug-resistant focal epilepsy because of its frequent ictal involvement. However, frequency-specific and directional thalamocortical dynamics remain poorly understood. We sought to characterize thalamocortical network dynamics across seizure evolution using stereoelectroencephalography (SEEG). METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed SEEG recordings from patients with pediatric-onset drug-resistant focal epilepsy with simultaneous cortical and thalamic sampling focusing on the anterior nucleus (AN) and centromedian nucleus (CM). We applied visual analysis with quantitative measures of spectral power, imaginary coherence, and spectral Granger causality across slow (1-12 Hz), β (13-30 Hz), and γ (30-70 Hz) bands to quantify thalamocortical and cortico-cortical interactions from seizure onset to termination. Random forest models were trained to classify seizure state using thalamic EEG features as an exploratory analysis. RESULTS: The final cohort comprised 19 patients (median age, 16.9 years; 31.6% female), with 66 focal seizures analyzed. Thalamic ictal activity was present at seizure onset in 81% of sampled thalamic contacts and became nearly universal by seizure termination. Both the thalamus and seizure onset zone showed increased spectral power during seizures, with prominent slow-band augmentation at seizure termination in the thalamus. Similarly, thalamocortical imaginary coherence increased during seizures, with the largest changes near seizure termination in the slow band (AN-SOZ: 11% [IQR, 2 to 20], rank-biserial correlations (rrb) = 0.84; CM-SOZ: 12% [IQR, 3 to 23], rrb = 0.84) and β band (AN-SOZ: 5% [IQR, 0 to 12], rrb = 0.74; CM-SOZ: 8% [IQR, 2 to 15], rrb = 0.86). Directed connectivity analyses demonstrated bidirectional thalamocortical interactions, with large slow-frequency thalamus-to-SOZ outflow near seizure termination from both AN and CM, exceeding propagation-zone-to-SOZ cortico-cortical flow (both p < 0.01). Seizure state was classified using thalamic EEG features, with area under the receiver operating characteristic curve values of 0.825 for AN and 0.839 for CM. DISCUSSION: Human focal seizures engage frequency-specific and bidirectional thalamocortical interactions that evolve from onset to termination. These findings support the thalamus as a source of clinically relevant seizure-state information and may inform thalamic biomarker development for neuromodulation in drug-resistant epilepsy.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Cenobamate (CNB) is a novel antiseizure medication with a dual mechanism of action that has demonstrated high efficacy in drug-resistant focal epilepsy; however, evidence in brain tumor-related epilepsy (BTRE) remains limited. The study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of CNB as add-on therapy in a multicenter cohort of patients with BTRE. METHODS: We conducted a multicenter, retrospective observational study across tertiary epilepsy centers in the Lazio region, Italy. Adult patients with drug-resistant BTRE treated with CNB as adjunctive therapy were included. Seizure frequency was assessed at baseline and during follow-up. Primary outcomes were changes in monthly seizure frequency and responder rates. Effectiveness was analyzed in both the overall population and in a homogeneous cohort of patients with 6-month follow-up. RESULTS: Twenty patients (10 females, median age 48 years) were included. Tumor histology included meningioma (n = 7, 35%), astrocytoma (n = 4, 20%), glioblastoma (n = 3, 15%), ganglioglioma (n = 3, 15%), oligodendroglioma (n = 2, 10%), and dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor (n = 1, 5%). Responder rates were 80% (≥50% seizure reduction), 60% (≥75%), and 20% achieved seizure freedom. Adverse events occurred in 12 patients (60%), including sedation (n = 8, 40%), postural instability (n = 3, 15%), diarrhea, and mild cutaneous rash (n = 1, 5% each). CNB was discontinued in one patient (5%). At their last follow-up, 16 had at least half as many seizures, and four were seizure-free. Twelve patients experienced side effects, most commonly sleepiness; one stopped cenobamate because of a rash.
