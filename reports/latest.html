摘要：In a 2010 book chapter on the pioneering encephalographer Mary A.B. Brazier, PhD (1904-1995), a photo of a smiling woman is labeled "Margaret Lennox-Buchthal, wife of Fritz, daughter of William Lennox." Yet, like most women, Margaret Lennox-Buchthal, MD (1913-2001), was more than a wife and a daughter. She was an assistant professor at the Yale School of Medicine and associate professor at the University of Copenhagen Institute of Neurophysiology, and she was editor-in-chief of the journal Epilepsia (1969-1975). Her work had a lasting impact on our current concept of and approach to febrile seizures. She advocated for people with epilepsy and challenged stigmas as an educated divorced woman with a diagnosis of epilepsy. The story of Lennox-Buchthal's life illustrates the unsung roles women played in medicine and research in the mid-1900s, and how their achievements continue to be eclipsed by the men in their lives.

摘要：BACKGROUND: Drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE) remains a critical challenge in pediatric neurology, profoundly impacting cognitive development and health-related quality of life (HRQOL). Given the role of the gut-microbiota-brain axis in neuromodulation, probiotics have emerged as a potential adjunctive therapy. This study assessed changes in seizure frequency and HRQOL and evaluated the safety of Lactobacillus reuteri supplementation on seizure frequency and HRQOL in children with DRE. METHODS: In this prospective, single-arm, before-after interventional trial, 75 children (aged 3-18 years) with confirmed DRE were enrolled. Participants received daily oral L. reuteri (1 ×10 9 CFU) for 12 weeks alongside their stable anti-seizure medication (ASM) regimens. The primary outcome was HRQOL measured by the Persian-validated QOLCE-55 questionnaire. Secondary outcomes included seizure frequency, recorded prospectively using standardized caregiver seizure diaries. Statistical analyses utilized paired-sample t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, with effect sizes calculated to quantify the magnitude of within-participant changes. RESULTS: Seventy-one patients completed the study (mean age 7.26 ± 3.33 years; 53.5% male). The cohort showed heterogeneous seizure phenotypes and underlying etiologies. Total HRQOL scores increased from 33.75 ± 4.92 at baseline to 34.23 ± 4.97 after the intervention (absolute change, 0.48 points; p < 0.001), with a small-to-moderate standardized effect size (d = 0.40). However, this change was substantially below the 10-13-point MCID range used in the sample-size rationale. Median seizure frequency changed from 32 (IQR: 15-84) to 30 (IQR: 14-80) episodes per 3 months (p = 0.001; r = 0.39). Four patients (5.6%; 95% CI, 1.6-13.8%) experienced a reduction of more than 50% in seizure frequency. Adverse events were reported in 11.3% of participants and were mild and transient, with no treatment discontinuations. CONCLUSIONS: In this single-arm before-after study, statistically significant within-participant changes in HRQOL and seizure frequency were observed during the 12-week period following adjunctive L. reuteri supplementation. However, the absolute magnitude of these changes was modest and their clinical significance remains uncertain. Because there was no concurrent placebo control group, the observed changes cannot be attributed specifically to L. reuteri supplementation. Larger, adequately powered placebo-controlled randomized trials are needed to determine whether L. reuteri provides a clinically meaningful benefit in pediatric drug-resistant epilepsy.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Sleep-related hypermotor epilepsy (SHE) is classically considered a frontal lobe epilepsy; however, accumulating evidence suggests that hypermotor seizures may arise from multiple frontal and extrafrontal regions. METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed 17 patients with clinically diagnosed SHE who underwent comprehensive evaluation, including long-term video-electroencephalographic (EEG) monitoring, 3-T high-resolution magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography, and, where indicated, magnetoencephalography, stereo-EEG (SEEG), and surgical assessment.
