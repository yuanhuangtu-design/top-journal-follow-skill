摘要：OBJECTIVE: Psychoses of epilepsy (POE) affect up to 13% of patients with focal epilepsy. Whereas interictal risk factors are well documented, ictal correlates of POE remain poorly characterized. We hypothesized that an impairment of ictal consciousness on the Consciousness Seizure Scale (CSS), would be associated with POE. We assessed the association between ictal clinical features and POE compared to non-POE in adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed a presurgical cohort of adults with drug-resistant focal epilepsy monitored by video-electroencephalography (EEG) or stereo-EEG. For each patient, all seizures with an examination allowing the completion of CSS score were included. POE were identified through standardized psychiatric interviews. Association between ictal features, primarily CSS score, and POE was tested using generalized estimating equations (GEE). RESULTS: Among 232 included seizures (25 from POE group, 207 from non-POE group), the probability of POE varied nonlinearly with ictal consciousness; it peaked at intermediate CSS scores (GEE, OR = 4.31, p = .006). This effect remained after adjustment for duration of both epilepsy (OR=1.06, p = .005) and seizure (OR=1.20, p = .013). Intraclass correlation of CSS was .76 (95%CI, .65-.85), reflecting a low within-patient variance. An older age, more severe depressive and anxiety symptoms were also associated with POE. SIGNIFICANCE: POE were associated with intermediate impairment of ictal consciousness, suggesting that partial disruption of awareness may favor the emergence of psychotic symptoms. The high level of intrapatient stability for CSS, mostly estimated from non-POE, suggests that CSS could be constant across seizures. This raises the possibility that POE risk could stem from the way consciousness is altered during seizures.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: To characterize serum and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) cytokine dynamics according to immunotherapy exposure in cryptogenic new-onset refractory status epilepticus (cNORSE). METHODS: We analyzed cytokine profiles in 94 serum and 42 CSF samples from 29 and 14 patients, respectively, with cNORSE. Nine contributed both sample types, resulting in 34 patients. Samples were categorized according to immunotherapy exposure as no immunotherapy (NT), first-line (FL), and second-line (SL), and stratified by innate- or adaptive-directed therapies. SL represented cumulative exposure, as all SL samples followed initiation of both FL and SL therapies. Linear mixed-effects models accounted for repeated measures and time from cNORSE onset. A systematic literature review included 27 studies and 63 patients. RESULTS: Serum cytokine levels were generally lower during FL (n = 24) than NT (n = 9), whereas most cytokines were higher during SL (n = 61) than FL. CXCL8 (C-X-C motif ligand 8)/IL-8 and CCL2 (C-C motif chemokine ligand 2) were higher during SL than FL, with CCL2 remaining significant after multiple-testing correction. No serum differences remained significant when analyses were restricted to samples collected 7-30 days after onset. CSF levels were generally lower during FL (n = 10) and SL (n = 25) than NT (n = 7). Innate-directed therapies were associated with higher serum IL-6, CXCL8/IL-8, and CCL2 and CSF IL-4, whereas adaptive-directed therapies were associated with lower CSF MIP-1α (Macrophage Inflammatory Protein-1 alpha). The association with higher serum IL-6 was no longer observed after excluding tocilizumab-treated patients. No temporal association after SL initiation or status epilepticus resolution remained significant after multiple-testing correction. The literature review showed more frequent reductions in pro-inflammatory cytokines in CSF than serum following immunotherapy initiation. SIGNIFICANCE: Cytokine profiles in cNORSE differ by treatment exposure and compartment. Persistent serum inflammation during SL may reflect disease severity, systemic inflammation, treatment effects, or their combination, whereas CSF showed more consistent reductions after immunotherapy exposure. These findings highlight the importance of treatment timing and compartment when interpreting cytokine measurements and support prospective studies integrating baseline immune phenotypes and standardized clinical outcomes.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Rare de novo variants in GNAO1 cause a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by a heterogenous display of early onset epilepsy, movement abnormalities, and global developmental delay. More than 100 pathogenic variants in GNAO1 have been identified and categorized into four groups according to their protein features. Most pathogenic variants affect both GNAO1 isoforms, whereas approximately 20% of patients have a GNAO1A-specific variant. Response to therapy is notoriously variable, even among patients harboring the same variant. Here, we set out to model GNAO1A-related encephalopathy using human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) from monozygotic twins carrying the Gα o1 K278del variant and uncovered-while taking advantage of the identical genomic setting-phenotypic features that further our understanding of the defects underlying the disorder. METHODS: We generated hiPSCs expressing the c.832_834delAAG; p.K278del variant in GNAO1A obtained from monozygotic twins who both developed seizures 2 days after birth. For either twin, Gα o1 K278del and CRISPR-Cas9-repaired isogenic control hiPSC lines were differentiated into neural progenitor cells (NPCs) and subsequently into cortical neurons. Variant-specific transcriptomic, morphological, and electrophysiological characteristics were analyzed. RESULTS: Gα o1 K278del mutated hiPSC-derived NPCs showed disorganized neural rosette structures and revealed an altered transcriptome. In cortical neurons, the Gα o1 K278del variant caused a reduction in the size and complexity of dendrites that was accompanied by an increase in pre- and postsynaptic puncta. Multielectrode array (MEA) recordings revealed an abnormal cortical neuron bursting activity pattern with fewer bursts and more spikes per burst. The antiepileptic drug levetiracetam reduced the spike frequency and the number of spikes per burst in cortical neurons from both patients. SIGNIFICANCE: The developed GNAO1A hiPSC-derived in vitro model uncovered key structural and functional features of GNAO1-related encephalopathy. The comparable in vitro phenotypes in neurons from both twins underscore the utility of hiPSC-derived models to investigate molecular mechanisms underlying GNAO1-related encephalopathy as well as effects of therapeutic intervention.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: To describe treatment decisions after first anti-seizure medication (ASM) failure in focal epilepsy and describe how they have changed across two trial eras, and whether observed prescribing matched clinicians stated preferences. METHODS: We analyzed the focal epilepsy cohorts from two UK pragmatic, randomized trials, Standard and New Antiepileptic Drugs (SANAD) (1999-2006) and SANAD II (2012-2019). Only initial ASM allocation was randomized; subsequent decisions reflected usual care, with medication changes and reason recorded prospectively. We reconstructed treatment sequences, classified first decisions after failure as switch or add-on, and stratified by reason, and compared prescribing with cross-sectional survey of UK epilepsy specialists. RESULTS: Of those enrolled, 1644 of 1721 (95.5%) in SANAD and 933 of 990 (94.2%) in SANAD II were included. First-line ASM failed in 798 (48.5%) and 360 (38.6%), respectively. Among classifiable failures from inadequate seizure control (ISC), add-on was 40.9% (79/193) in SANAD and 63.1% (82/130) in SANAD II. After lamotrigine failure, the only randomized ASM in both trials, add-on was 44.4% (16/36) in SANAD to 74.3% (26/35) in SANAD II. Forty-six specialists completed the survey. Their preferred next ASM broadly matched SANAD II prescribing: after lamotrigine failure from ISC, 74% (31/42) chose levetiracetam, prescribed to 66% (23/35); after levetiracetam failure, 51% (21/41) chose lamotrigine, prescribed to 52% (26/50). Strategy preferences differed: respondents favored switching after each specified failure, yet add-on predominated after ISC in SANAD II. SIGNIFICANCE: Add-on treatment after first ASM failure from ISC was more common in the later trial. Clinician-preferred next ASMs were consistent with observed practice, but preferred strategy did not. Neither trial was designed to compare these strategies, and cohorts differed in baseline characteristics and completeness of recorded reason for failure. Randomized evidence is needed to determine whether switching or add-on yields better seizure control, tolerability, and treatment-retention outcomes.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Intracranial electroencephalography (iEEG) practices and diagnostic testing vary among institutions, limiting standardization and optimization of surgical outcomes. This study estimates resection rates and surgical outcome effect sizes after iEEG and develops individual patient-level data (IPD) models using the missing-indicator method to approximate real-world clinical uncertainties and practice variability in iEEG and surgical decision-making. METHODS: Databases were queried for studies reporting ≥12-month outcomes after epilepsy surgery in patients with drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE) who underwent iEEG. Random-effects meta-analysis estimated pooled outcome proportions. IPD analysis was performed using multivariable generalized linear regression and logistic regression models with cross-validation and calibration assessment. Missing values for key predictors were retained as a "not specified" category to evaluate the impact of incorporating uncertainty in common clinical data on outcome prediction. RESULTS: Primary outcomes were seizure freedom (Engel class IA or International League Against Epilepsy [ILAE] 1) and favorable seizure outcomes (Engel I or ILAE 1-2). Of 4975 studies identified, 115 met the inclusion criteria. The pooled frequency of patients undergoing resection after iEEG was 67% (95% confidence interval [CI] 60%-74%), 52% achieved seizure freedom (95% CI 48%-56%), and 58% achieved a favorable outcome (95% CI 54%-62%). IPD analysis included 1305 patients from 29 studies and identified a lesional magnetic resonance imaging (MRI; odds ratio [OR] 1.94, CI 1.48-2.55, p < .0001) and a cortical malformation (OR 1.47, CI 1.08-2.00, p = .01) as independent predictors of a favorable outcome, whereas iEEG implantation strategy (bilateral implantation OR .61, 95% CI .42-.89, p = .01) was an independent predictor of a less favorable outcome. SIGNIFICANCE: About two-thirds of patients undergoing iEEG proceeded to resection, and about half achieved seizure freedom. Multivariable models incorporating clinical uncertainty and practice variability were well calibrated.
