An amygdala-mediated simulation-to-decision cascade aligns interbrain dynamics to solve social dilemmas.推荐

PMID 42897303 · Nature neuroscience · 2026 Oct 19
作者：Ni J, Meng F, Zhang C, Tan H et al.
链接：PubMed · DOI

摘要：Mutual cooperation (MC) requires independent decision-makers to coordinate under uncertainty about each other's intentions. Using simultaneous intracranial electroencephalography from epilepsy-patient dyads playing a real-time iterated prisoner's dilemma, we identified neurocognitive mechanisms that enable interacting brains to align during mutual cooperation. Amygdala interbrain representational alignment (IBRA), similarity in fine-grained neural spectral patterns across partners, emerged selectively during MC and predicted trial-by-trial outcomes. Early θ-suppression-characterized IBRA reflected rapid, history-sensitive alignment, whereas later IBRA, characterized by low-frequency suppression and γ increases, supported coordinated commitment and action. Furthermore, mutual cooperation relied on an amygdala-mediated, simulation-to-decision cascade, operationalized here as a model-based prospective estimate of partner cooperation that guided self-choice. Amygdala low-frequency suppression tracked this prospective estimate of partner cooperation and θ-γ coupling linked this partner estimate to self-decision processes. Conditional cooperators strategically relied on partner tracking, whereas unconditional cooperators leveraged dispositional tolerance of uncertainty to transition into anticipatory neural alignment. These findings reveal the neural interdependency underpinning MC, redefining cooperation not as isolated decision-making but as a dynamic, interbrain, cognitive alignment.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: To determine if the spatial extent of interictal fast ripples in focal epilepsy patients is associated with brain dysfunction. METHODS: Thirty pediatric focal epilepsy patients who underwent SEEG investigation had their cognitive and language deficits assessed in scales of 0 to 4. Fast ripples (250-500 Hz), ripples (80-250 Hz), and IEDs were automatically detected in 10 min during NREM sleep. We fitted linear models to the relationship between the cognitive deficit scale and the spatial spread of the interictal neurophysiologic events (number of channels with rate of events > 10 % of maximum rate). For the language deficit, we computed the Spearman correlation between the median rate of events in all brain regions and the deficit scale. RESULTS: Cognitive deficit scale was significantly associated with the number of channels with fast ripples (p = 0.049), not so with ripples (p = 0.31), IEDs (p = 0.31). However, from all brain regions the most statistically significant correlations (p < 0.01) were between median fast ripple rates in the left and right pars opercularis and triangularis of the inferior frontal gyrus (Broca's area and its contralateral counterpart) with r = 0.66, p = 0.003 (18 patients), in the superior temporal gyrus in the language dominant hemisphere with r = 0.72, p = 0.005 (13 patients), and the dominant basal temporal region with r = 0.79, p = 0.006 (10 patients). Median rates of IEDs and ripples exhibited lower correlation with language deficits in these regions. CONCLUSION: Widespread fast ripples are associated with increased cognitive deficits, and language deficiencies are correlated with fast ripple rates in brain regions associated with language. The extent of fast-ripple activity was associated with cognitive deficit severity in this cohort, whereas the corresponding associations for ripples and IEDs did not reach statistical significance.

摘要：Medication adherence is a major determinant of seizure control in people with epilepsy, yet nonadherence remains frequent and is associated with breakthrough seizures, increased healthcare utilization, and higher mortality. Despite the central role of antiseizure medications (ASMs), adherence is a multifactorial behavior shaped by routine stability, health beliefs, self-efficacy, mood, and social support, and it cannot be improved by prescription alone. Physical exercise has emerged as a promising complementary strategy in the management of chronic diseases, with benefits that extend beyond general health. This short communication examines the potential of regular physical exercise to act as an adjunct to the pharmacological treatment of epilepsy, with particular emphasis on how it may favor medication adherence. At the behavioral level, physical exercise may stabilize daily routines, improve mood and self-efficacy, and reinforce treatment engagement factors that plausibly support consistent medication taking. At the physiological level, physical exercise modulates hemodynamic, metabolic, and neurobiological pathways, although direct evidence of clinically relevant effects on ASM disposition remains scarce. Nonetheless, exercise-induced pharmacokinetic alterations depend on the type, intensity, and duration of exertion-ranging from sustained aerobic exercise to high-intensity resistance training-which differentially modulate hemodynamics, metabolic pathways, and plasma protein binding, with the greatest potential relevance for antiseizure medications with a narrow therapeutic index. Barriers to physical exercise participation, including fear of seizures, stigma, and limited professional counseling, parallel those that compromise adherence and deserve systematic attention. We conclude that physical exercise should be regarded as a safe, low-cost, complementary component of epilepsy care, and that prospective studies examining its effects on medication adherence and pharmacological outcomes represent a research priority.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Generalized spike-wave discharges (GSWD) are occasionally observed in patients with self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes (SeLECTS), but their clinical significance remains unclear. This study aimed to characterize the electroclinical features of these patients and to explore their potential clinical implications. METHODS: We retrospectively reviewed the clinical data of 148 children diagnosed with SeLECTS at Xuanwu Hospital from June 2015 to October 2025. Patients with GSWD on electroencephalography were identified and compared with those without GSWD regarding demographic characteristics, seizure semiology, auxiliary examination findings, treatment response, and outcomes. RESULTS: GSWD were identified in 9 of 148 patients (6.1 %) with SeLECTS. These 9 patients included 4 males and 5 females, with a median onset age of 8 (IQR: 5-10) years. 摘要：BACKGROUND: Severe disruption of cortico-thalamocortical circuits underlies disorders of consciousness (DOC). Beta-band (20 Hz) transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) can be used to probe frontal oscillatory synchrony, yet its potential to acutely modulate large-scale network dynamics in DOC is unknown. METHODS: We applied 20 Hz tACS (1 mA) for 30 min over the frontal cortex (F3 and F4, International 10-20 system) in 28 patients with DOC. Offline functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) was used to quantify hemoglobin (HbO)-based functional connectivity (FC) and graph-theoretical network properties, including small-world properties (clustering coefficient, C p ; path length, L p ) and global efficiency (global efficiency, E g ; local efficiency, E loc ) at pre-stimulation resting-state measurement for 5 min (pre-stim), the first post-stimulation resting-state measurement for 5 min (post-stim1), and the second post-stimulation resting-state measurement for 5 min (post-stim2). Subgroups were defined by baseline video-electroencephalography (vEEG) pathology as moderately abnormal (n = 11) and diffuse slowing (n = 14); 3 patients excluded (incomplete vEEG). RESULTS: Frontal 20 Hz tACS was associated with transient enhancement of FC across the measured cortical network at post-stim1 (pFDR < 0.05), selectively strengthening intra-and interhemispheric and intra-nodal connectivity before resolving by post-stim2. Crucially, the effects were pathology-dependent, and visual-motor integration pathways (right occipital lobe to left motor cortex; right primary somatosensory cortex to left occipital lobe) emerged exclusively in DOC patients with moderately abnormal background but not in those with diffuse slowing. Concurrent acute network reorganization at post-stim1 (all p < 0.05) featured increased C p , E g , and E loc , along with decreased L p , all normalizing by post-stim2. Clinically, Coma Recovery Scale-Revised scores correlated positively with average FC of HbO (pre-stim: r = 0.47; post-stim2: r = 0.41) and Area Under the Curve (AUC) of E g (pre-stim: r = 0.44), and negatively with AUC of L p (pre-stim: r = - 0.41; all p < 0.05). CONCLUSIONS: Frontal beta-tACS was associated with state-dependent changes in functional network organization in DOC, with dynamic fNIRS measures that may be useful for tracking these changes. These findings provide preliminary descriptive evidence of short-term network changes associated with beta-tACS and may help guide future controlled studies of targeted neuromodulation in patients with DOC.

摘要：PURPOSE OF REVIEW: This review critically evaluates evidence on sleep spindles (SP), slow oscillations (SOs), and their coordination in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease (AD), focusing on their relationships with cognition, AD biomarkers, and their therapeutic potential. RECENT FINDINGS: Longitudinal studies associate greater SP and/or SO activity with better cognition and favourable amyloid and tau profiles after clinical onset. Spindle and SO coupling may index network integrity. Preserved NREM sleep oscillations are associated with lower orexin concentrations and weaker adverse relationships between orexin and cognitive and neuropsychiatric outcomes. Closed-loop acoustic stimulation and slow-oscillatory transcranial direct-current stimulation can modify SO activity, spindle activity, and coupling, although cognitive and biomarker benefits remain uncertain. Spindle-SO activity, including their temporal coupling, are associated with cognition and AD biomarkers across the disease continuum. Closed-loop acoustic and electrical stimulation can modify these oscillations, but biomarker-characterized trials in MCI and AD are needed to determine whether treatment produces durable cognitive and biomarker benefits.
Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) combined with antidepressant pharmacotherapy for depression: state-dependent mechanisms, clinical evidence, and translational challenges.必读

摘要：Depression is a highly prevalent and debilitating mental disorder. Pharmacotherapy remains the primary treatment modality; however, its efficacy is often limited by a delayed onset of action, suboptimal response in some patients, and a high risk of recurrence. Treatment-resistant depression, characterized by poor response to conventional therapies, poses a major clinical challenge and highlights the need for improved treatment strategies. Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), a noninvasive neuromodulation technique, has shown promise for depression, particularly when combined with antidepressant pharmacotherapy. Advances in neuroimaging, neurochemistry, and network neuroscience have clarified how high-frequency rTMS may modulate cortical excitability, monoaminergic transmission, synaptic plasticity, and distributed brain networks. Clinical evidence suggests that combined treatment may accelerate early improvement, increase response and remission, and reduce relapse risk; however, these benefits are heterogeneous across patients and symptom domains and should be interpreted in light of expectancy effects and limitations of sham blinding. Neuroimaging studies have identified treatment-associated functional and structural changes, including hippocampal volume changes, but the causal links among molecular signaling, circuit reorganization, structural findings, and symptom improvement remain unresolved. The interaction between rTMS and antidepressants should therefore not be viewed as uniformly additive or synergistic, but as a state-dependent process shaped by medication class, stimulation protocol and timing, cortical excitability, metaplasticity, and individual neurobiological context. This review integrates mechanistic, neuroimaging, and clinical evidence and discusses how biomarker-, state-, and symptom-informed targeting, optimized treatment sequencing, and pragmatic implementation strategies could improve the precision and accessibility of combined treatment for depression.
