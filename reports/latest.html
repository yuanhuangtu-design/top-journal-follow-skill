摘要：BACKGROUND: Progressive myoclonic epilepsies (PMEs) are rare neurodegenerative disorders characterized by cortical hyperexcitability and progressive neurological deterioration. We aimed to identify electrophysiological markers of cortical dysfunction using multimodal neurophysiological assessment. METHODS: Seventeen adult PME patients underwent clinical evaluation and video-EEG recording. Somatosensory evoked potentials (SEP), SEP recovery function and surround inhibition, blink reflex, and motor evoked potentials (MEP) were assessed in 11 patients and compared with 11 matched healthy controls. RESULTS: Generalized epileptiform discharges were observed in all patients. SEP recordings demonstrated increased N20/P25 amplitudes and prolonged P14, N18, and P25 latencies. SEP recovery showed marked attenuation at the shortest interstimulus interval followed by rapid amplitude recovery. Surround inhibition was preserved. Blink reflex studies revealed prolonged R2 latencies and increased amplitudes. MEP parameters did not differ significantly between groups. CONCLUSIONS: PME is associated with abnormal SEP recovery dynamics and giant SEPs, consistent with impaired somatosensory inhibitory processing. Blink reflex abnormalities suggest additional subcortical involvement, whereas corticospinal excitability appears relatively preserved.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: This study was undertaken to evaluate whether roseola infantum is associated with an increased risk of subsequent epilepsy. METHODS: We conducted a nationwide retrospective cohort study using the Korean National Health Insurance Service database from January 2002 to December 2021. Children with roseola, identified using International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) code B08.2, were matched 1:1 by age and sex with children without roseola. Participants were followed from the date of roseola diagnosis, or the corresponding index date for controls, until incident epilepsy, death, or the end of the study period. The primary epilepsy definition required at least two medical encounters with ICD-10 code G40.x listed among the top five discharge diagnoses and cumulative antiseizure medication prescriptions for 180 days or longer. Sensitivity analyses used a more inclusive epilepsy definition. RESULTS: The cohort included 1 595 494 children: 797 747 with roseola and 797 747 matched controls. Postindex febrile convulsion showed the strongest independent association with epilepsy (adjusted HR = 12.31, 95% CI = 11.82-12.83). 摘要：Accurate assessment of seizure occurrence is central to treatment optimization, risk stratification, and management of epilepsy, yet remains largely dependent on patient-reported seizure diaries, which are frequently incomplete and unreliable. Wearable technologies have emerged as promising tools to enable objective and continuous seizure monitoring by capturing seizure-induced physiological signals. Over the past decade, substantial progress has been made in the development and clinical validation of wearable seizure detection systems, particularly for generalized convulsive seizures, where high sensitivity can be achieved, whereas detection of nonconvulsive seizures remains more limited. However, translating detection into clinically meaningful seizure quantification presents important challenges, including variability in performance across seizure types, patient populations, and individual physiological responses, as well as differences between controlled and real-world settings. This narrative review critically examines wearable technologies for seizure quantification, integrating recent clinical evidence and expert perspectives. We discuss key sensor modalities, including accelerometry, electrocardiography-derived features such as heart rate variability, multimodal biosignal integration, and emerging ultra-long-term wearable and subcutaneous electroencephalographic systems. Each modality offers specific advantages and limitations depending on seizure type and clinical context. Attention is also given to the importance of user trust, adherence, and data quality as key determinants of clinical utility. We further examine the role of wearable seizure quantification in clinical practice, including treatment monitoring, tele-epileptology, and clinical trials, as well as potential future contributions to sudden unexpected death in epilepsy risk assessment. With continued technological advances and robust real-world clinical validation, wearable systems have the potential to transform seizure monitoring and support more precise, data-driven epilepsy care.

链接：PubMed · DOI

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Accurate localization of the seizure onset zone (SOZ) is critical for the presurgical evaluation of drug-resistant epilepsy. Intracranial electroencephalography captures high-resolution seizure dynamics, but interpreting complex spatiotemporal patterns across implanted electrodes is clinically challenging. We propose a two-stage, uncertainty-aware framework for SOZ localization that integrates patient-specific seizure modeling with cross-patient probabilistic ranking to estimate the likelihood of each electrode belonging to the SOZ. METHODS: The framework was evaluated on a bi-institutional cohort of 36 patients (20 with good surgical outcome, 16 with poor surgical outcome), comprising 184 seizures. In the first stage, a patient-specific spatiotemporal graph neural network modeled seizure onset as a network-level transition using directed functional connectivity graphs derived from burst-integrated electrophysiological features. Trained solely to detect preictal-to-ictal transitions, this model generated electrode probability trajectories without SOZ supervision. In the second stage, these trajectories were summarized into interpretable descriptors and used to train a patient-agnostic classifier assigning SOZ likelihood scores to electrodes. Evaluation and hyperparameter optimization were performed using nested leave-one-patient-out cross-validation restricted to good outcome patients, ensuring strict patient-level separation between training, validation, and test data. RESULTS: Seizure onset detection was stable across patients, with comparable binary cross-entropy values between outcome groups (median = .495 vs. .438, p = .108). In good outcome patients, SOZ electrodes were prioritized among top-ranked contacts (area under the curve = .86 ± .06), with all patients exhibiting at least one SOZ electrode within the top positions. In poor outcome patients, predictions exhibited reduced spatial separability, lower likelihood magnitudes, and near-zero confidence gaps, reflecting attenuated model confidence. SIGNIFICANCE: This two-stage framework integrates spatial interactions, temporal dynamics, and probabilistic ranking within a pipeline. By providing graded SOZ likelihood estimates and uncertainty-aware prioritization, the approach is a decision-support tool to assist hypothesis refinement during presurgical evaluation rather than as a standalone diagnostic system.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Mild malformation of cortical development with oligodendroglial hyperplasia and epilepsy (MOGHE) is a recently recognized cause of drug-resistant focal epilepsy. It is often magnetic resonance imaging-negative or radiologically resembles focal cortical dysplasias, which makes recognition difficult and limits presurgical counseling. We aimed to identify an intracranial electroencephalographic (iEEG) biomarker that distinguishes MOGHE from other developmental brain lesions encountered in epilepsy surgery. METHODS: We analyzed long-term stereo-EEG and subdural recordings in a retrospective multicenter test cohort (18 MOGHE, 20 non-MOGHE) to identify an iEEG pattern distinguishing MOGHE from other developmental brain lesions. We then evaluated its diagnostic performance by blinded visual assessment in an independent single-center validation cohort (11 MOGHE, 11 non-MOGHE). Firth-penalized multivariable logistic regression tested whether MOGHE histology independently predicted the presence of this pattern after adjustment for spasms, frontal localization, age at seizure onset, and age at implantation. Finally, within MOGHE, we assessed whether resection of the territory exhibiting this pattern was associated with postsurgical seizure freedom. RESULTS: We identified highly stereotyped clusters of very brief low-voltage fast activity (LVFA), recurring in statuslike episodes lasting several minutes and sometimes lacking clear clinical symptoms. LVFA clusters occurred in 16 of 18 MOGHE and in no non-MOGHE patients in the test cohort. In the validation cohort, visual identification of LVFA clusters correctly classified 10 of 11 MOGHE and 10 of 11 non-MOGHE cases (overall agreement = 90.9%, 95% Wilson confidence interval = 72.2%-97.5%). MOGHE histology was the dominant independent predictor of LVFA clusters. Within MOGHE, resection of LVFA cluster territory was associated with postsurgical seizure freedom (Mann-Whitney U-test, p = .0021).
