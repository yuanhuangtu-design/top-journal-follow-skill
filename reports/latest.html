摘要：Recent studies have identified bi-directional communication between neurons and glioma tumors as a key driver of hyperexcitability and malignant progression. Although much of this work has focused on the activity of excitatory neurons, the roles of inhibitory neurons remain poorly defined. Using in vivo screening approaches in autochthonous glioma models, we found that the loss of a γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) transporter (Slc6a1) enhanced glioma progression, promoted network hyperactivity, and influenced tonic GABA release by tumors. Mechanistically, we show that glioma tumors influence the activity of inhibitory neurons and that their subsequent stimulation suppresses tumor growth. Further screening of these phenomena identified diazepam binding inhibitor (DBI), a secreted negative allosteric modulator of GABA-A, as a link between glioma-related hyperexcitability and proliferation that functions through modulating inhibitory neuronal signaling. Collectively, our findings indicate that glioma tumors utilize multiple mechanisms to manipulate the activity of inhibitory neurons as a means to drive network hyperactivity, which ultimately facilitates malignant progression.

摘要：Pathogenic variants in KCNH1, encoding the human ether-á-go-go voltage-gated potassium channel (EAG1, KV10.1), are associated with the neurodevelopmental disorders Temple-Baraitser syndrome (TBS), Zimmermann-Laband syndrome (ZLS), epileptic encephalopathy, and other undefined neurodevelopmental disorders. Investigating the functional consequences of KCNH1 variants can provide information about molecular and cellular pathophysiological mechanisms and help guide therapeutic directions. In this study, we determined the functional properties of 26 disease-associated KCNH1 variants including three recurrent and six that were previously unpublished. Findings were compared to the wildtype (WT) channel along with six rare population variants. Our approach used automated patch clamp recording of heterologously expressed KCNH1 variants in both the homozygous state (variant alone) and the clinically-relevant heterozygous state (variant co-expressed with WT channels). We also examined the impact of alternative splice isoforms on functional consequences of specific variants. We demonstrated that the majority of disease-associated KCNH1 variants exhibit features consistent with gain-of-function including hyperpolarized voltage-dependence of activation, accelerated activation kinetics, or both that promote hyperpolarization of the resting membrane potential. By contrast, one unique variant discovered previously in an autism cohort had a depolarized voltage-dependence of activation and was associated with a depolarized resting potential in cells. Pharmacological testing of KCNH1 variants demonstrated differences in potency for channel block by imipramine and an investigational non-selective potassium channel blocker (LY97241) for specific variants. Collectively, our findings support gain-of-function as the most common, albeit not exclusive, molecular mechanism underlying KCNH1-related disorders and suggest opportunities for potential therapeutic drug repurposing strategies for these conditions.

摘要：BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: The World Health Organization's Intersectoral Global Action Plan on Epilepsy and other Neurologic Disorders 2022-2031 aims to minimize epilepsy burden, with an emphasis on Indigenous peoples. Māori, the Indigenous peoples of New Zealand, have a similar prevalence of treated epilepsy to non-Māori yet suffer an increased epilepsy burden. This study aims to describe epilepsy etiology, syndrome, and severity in Māori children compared with non-Māori-non-Pasifika children. METHODS: Retrospective population-based study using the New Zealand Pharmaceutical Collection. Medical records were reviewed to compare epilepsy etiology, type, syndrome, and severity between Māori and age-matched and sex-matched non-Māori-non-Pasifika children (younger than 18 years) with treated epilepsy. Period prevalence of etiology was compared: logistic regression (odds ratio, OR) for comparisons of within-cohort proportions and Poisson regression (rate ratio, RR) for comparison of population prevalence. RESULTS: Cohort of 337 Māori and 421 non-Māori-non-Pasifika children (female: 45.4% Māori and 46.6% non-Māori-non-Pasifika). The median age of first seizure was 3.0 years for Māori (interquartile range [IQR]: 0.9-8.0) and 4.0 years for non-Māori-non-Pasifika (IQR: 1.3-8.0). Māori children were more likely to live in high deprivation areas (quintile 5: 48.4% Māori vs 17.3% non-Māori-non-Pasifika). Māori children were more likely to have a preventable cause of their epilepsy than non-Māori-non-Pasifika children (RR: 2.32; 95% CI 1.41-3.80) and more likely to have a structural (RR: 1.43, 95% CI 1.04-1.97) or infectious (RR: 4.72, 95% CI 1.43-15.8) etiology. An epilepsy syndrome was identified in 26% of Māori and 36% of non-Māori-non-Pasifika children. Māori children were less likely to have a self-limited epilepsy syndrome (OR: 0.51, 95% CI 0.30-0.83) or developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (OR: 0.59, 95% CI 0.40-0.88) but more likely to have intellectual disability and epilepsy (OR: 1.65, 95% CI 1.18-2.32). DISCUSSION: Health inequities exist for Māori children who are twice as likely to have preventable epilepsy than non-Māori-non-Pasifika children. This inequity is largely driven by socioeconomic status and rurality.

摘要：IMPORTANCE: Functional seizures (FS) are a common and disabling presentation in neurology clinics, but evidence-based treatments are difficult to access in Australia and in many regions globally. A brief, scalable, internet-delivered intervention could narrow this treatment gap. OBJECTIVE: To compare a self-guided and a therapist-guided internet-based intervention for FS, each delivered alongside standard care, with standard care alone. DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This was a 3-arm, parallel, randomized clinical trial conducted between February 2022 and April 2024, with follow-up through to 12 months after randomization. The location was a specialist outpatient clinic in Melbourne, Australia. Included were adults (≥18 years) with a documented diagnosis of FS confirmed by video electroencephalogram monitoring and at least 4 seizures per month, excluding comorbid active epilepsy. INTERVENTIONS: Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to a self-guided internet-based intervention plus standard care, therapist-guided internet-based intervention plus standard care, or standard care alone. Re-PROGRAM is an internet-based intervention developed for FS, comprising 5 structured sessions integrating lifestyle factors, seizure management techniques, and elements of cognitive behavioral, acceptance and commitment, and dialectical behavior therapy. MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary outcome was monthly seizure frequency at 6 weeks, 6 months, and 12 months after randomization, recorded by daily prospective seizure diary, with retrospective estimates used for incomplete records. Secondary outcomes included depression, anxiety, quality of life, psychosocial functioning, and somatic symptoms. RESULTS: Of 220 individuals screened for eligibility, 107 (mean [SD] age 36.0 [12.4] years; 89 female [83%]) were randomized. Of these participants, 96 contributed to the full analysis set (29 self-guided, 34 therapist-guided, 33 standard care). The self-guided arm produced significantly greater overall reductions in monthly seizure frequency than standard care (rate ratio [RR], 0.35; 95% CI, 0.18-0.69; Holm-Bonferroni [HB] method P = .006), with sustained between-group effects at 6 weeks (RR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.18-0.76; HB P = .02), 6 months (RR, 0.31; 95% CI, 0.12-0.78; HB P = .04), and 12 months (RR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.16-0.78; HB P = .03). The self-guided arm also showed greater improvements in anxiety than standard care at all 3 time points and in depression and mental quality of life at 1 or more time points. The therapist-guided arm showed greater improvement in anxiety at 6 months, but reductions in seizure frequency did not reach statistical significance. Direct comparison between the 2 active arms showed no significant difference in seizure reduction (RR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.33-1.36). Sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness of the primary findings. No adverse events occurred in any arm. CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Results of this randomized clinical trial show that in adults with FS, a self-guided internet-based intervention reduced seizure frequency and improved anxiety relative to standard care, offering a scalable option for patients unable to access specialist psychological care. Comparative efficacy of first-line antiseizure monotherapy in pediatric focal epilepsy: An umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses.必读

PMID 42949619 · Epileptic disorders : international epilepsy journal with videotape · 2026 Oct 28
作者：Karaahmet AY, Bilgiç FŞ, Yüksel A
链接：PubMed · DOI
命中检索：癫痫专科刊 × Epilepsy
摘要：INTRODUCTION: First-line antiseizure medication (ASM) monotherapy remains the recommended initial treatment for pediatric focal epilepsy. Although several ASMs are available, uncertainty persists regarding their comparative efficacy, safety, and tolerability, particularly in children. This umbrella review aimed to synthesize and map the available evidence from systematic reviews and meta-analyses evaluating first-line ASM monotherapies for focal epilepsy, with a focus on informing pediatric treatment decisions. METHODS: This umbrella review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidance. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses evaluating first-line ASM monotherapy for focal epilepsy were identified through comprehensive database searches. Data on study characteristics, patient populations, ASM comparisons, epilepsy subtypes, follow-up duration, efficacy, safety, and tolerability outcomes were extracted and synthesized descriptively. Evidence mapping was performed to summarize the findings, and potential overlap of primary studies across the included reviews was considered. Where available, outcome-specific certainty-of-evidence ratings reported by the source reviews using GRADE or CINeMA were also extracted and summarized. RESULTS: Sixteen systematic reviews and meta-analyses were included, comprising randomized controlled trials and individual participant data analyses from pediatric and mixed-age populations. Frequently evaluated ASMs included carbamazepine, lamotrigine, levetiracetam, oxcarbazepine, valproate, phenytoin, and several newer-generation agents. Across the included reviews, no single first-line ASM showed consistent superiority for seizure control outcomes. Selected comparisons suggested tolerability advantages for lamotrigine, levetiracetam, and some newer-generation ASMs; however, these findings were not uniform across outcomes or reviews. Several comparative estimates were imprecise, with confidence intervals encompassing potentially clinically meaningful effects, and substantial heterogeneity was present across epilepsy subtype definitions, patient populations, follow-up durations, and outcome reporting. The frequent inclusion of mixed-age populations further limited the strength and direct applicability of pediatric-specific conclusions. CONCLUSIONS: Current umbrella-level evidence does not establish clear superiority of one first-line ASM over another for seizure control in pediatric focal epilepsy. Importantly, the absence of statistically significant differences should not be interpreted as evidence of therapeutic equivalence, because several estimates remain imprecise and clinically meaningful differences cannot be excluded. Interpretation is further limited by the scarcity of pediatric-specific comparative data, the frequent inclusion of mixed-age populations, and methodological and outcome heterogeneity across reviews.
