摘要：PURPOSE: The temporal development of generalized paroxysmal fast activity (GPFA) and slow spike-and-wave complexes in childhood Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (LGS) remains poorly characterized. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. RESULTS: We reviewed 234 EEGs from 51 patients (75% male). Median age at first EEG was 5 years. GPFA emerged later than slow spike-and-wave (mean age: 9.6 vs 8.3 years, p = 0.002), with non-parametric analysis confirming a significant difference in age at first detection (p = 0.004). CONCLUSION: EEG hallmarks of LGS emerged progressively during childhood, with slow spike-and-wave preceding GPFA during sleep. 摘要：A new type of epilepsy syndrome, an etiology-specific epilepsy syndrome, was defined in 2022 in recognition of major advances in identifying the many genetic and acquired etiologies underlying the epilepsies. An etiology-specific epilepsy syndrome refers to where a known etiology is associated with a distinctive epilepsy presentation, including age at seizure onset, seizure types, and other morbidities, such as learning difficulties or intellectual disability, psychiatric features, gut and musculoskeletal problems. However, as we understand more about a specific etiology, such as a gene, it is clear that many etiologies are associated not just with one epilepsy syndrome, but with a range of epilepsy syndromes. Different epilepsy syndromes due to an abnormality in the same gene may reflect different types of dysfunction caused by the specific pathogenic genetic variant. Here, we expand the concept because-knowing the genetic etiology is not enough to ensure optimal clinical care-it requires a more nuanced approach to ensure the patient is managed appropriately. We define the concept of an etiology-specific spectrum of epilepsy syndromes (ESSES), which incorporates understanding the range of epilepsy syndromes that may be associated with a specific etiology. In some cases, a genetic etiology may be associated with a functional spectrum, such as pathogenic variants in an ion channel subunit that cause loss-of-function, gain-of-function, or mixed functional effects, each associated with specific epilepsy syndromes that may require different treatment approaches. With other genes, the functional effect may not yet be known, but their spectrum of epilepsy syndromes may be well established through genotype-phenotype studies. The concept of ESSES has the flexibility to grow in its scope with new insights gained from understanding the breadth of epilepsy syndromes associated with a specific etiology and the associated functional impact.

摘要：Some patients with hyperkinetic movement disorders may present with a severe and prolonged state of uncontrolled chorea and/or ballism, termed 'choreic storm', causing distress and prompting intervention. These events may be more frequent and severe in patients with childhood-onset movement disorders. Deep brain stimulation (DBS) can be effective for medically refractory dyskinesias including chorea, and the availability of event-based local field potential (LFP) sensing in clinically available devices present an opportunity to better understand the underlying neurophysiology of these episodic phenomena. We analyzed clinical, electrophysiological, and corroborating video data from a patient with a de novo CEP85L mutation (NM_001042475.2, c.2381A>G, p.Y794C) presenting with chorea-predominant dyskinetic cerebral palsy, microcephaly, and intellectual disability. The patient underwent bilateral pallidal DBS for medically refractory symptoms and showed improvement in her baseline chorea. However, choreic storms continued multiple times per week, requiring frequent rescue medications and/or emergency medical intervention. These episodes were associated with autonomic features and variable triggers. Family-initiated LFP event recording supplemented by concurrent video facilitated side-by-side analysis of clinical episodes, revealing elevated LFP power in the theta to low gamma frequencies during choreic storms compared to baseline event recordings. Episode-associated increases in LFP power captured during choreic storms may represent a putative within-patient electrophysiological correlate of these severe episodic exacerbations. Although requiring confirmation and extension in larger series, these observations suggest that adaptive stimulation paradigms could be useful for patients experiencing choreic storms.
