摘要：The ability to learn environmental regularities - statistical learning - is crucial for language, cognition and behaviour. Despite decades of research, fundamental questions remain about what statistical learning is, how the mind supports it and where in the brain it is implemented. We address these questions with a framework based on Marr's levels of analysis. At the 'what' level, we suggest that statistical learning is best defined in terms of its goal: to learn latent structure in the input across repeated exposures. We focus on three widely studied sequential statistical learning tasks that embody this definition: embedded pattern, artificial grammar and serial reaction time tasks. At the 'how' and 'where' levels, our comprehensive reviews reveal that sequential statistical learning depends on at least two cognitive learning processes (prediction-based learning and chunking) and two well-established neural learning circuits (rooted in the basal ganglia and the medial temporal lobe). We describe empirically motivated 'many-to-many' mappings between the tasks, learning processes and neural circuits that integrate the three levels. Further, we suggest that task, input and learner characteristics can shift the relative reliance on these processes and circuits. Overall, our synthesis elucidates the cognitive and neural substrates of sequential statistical learning and provides predictions to guide future research.

摘要：The peripheral nervous system has emerged as an important regulator of cancer biology. Once considered passive bystanders, tumour-infiltrating neurites are now recognized as active components of the tumour microenvironment that influence tumour progression through direct and indirect interactions with malignant, immune, stromal, glial and vascular cells. Recent studies have demonstrated that peripheral tumours recruit and remodel infiltrating neurites, inducing transcriptional, structural and functional changes that resemble neural injury and regenerative programs. These reprogrammed neurites release neurotransmitters, neuropeptides and other factors that directly promote tumour cell proliferation, migration and survival while reshaping the surrounding microenvironment. Neural signalling influences antitumour immunity, reprogrammes fibroblasts and promotes angiogenesis, collectively generating conditions that support disease progression. In turn, tumour-associated neurites undergo profound alterations in their connectivity, activity and molecular state, highlighting the dynamic and reciprocal nature of tumour-nerve interactions. These discoveries have reshaped our understanding of how pro-tumorigenic microenvironments are established and maintained. Importantly, defining the molecular pathways that mediate neural regulation of cancer has revealed new therapeutic opportunities, including the potential repurposing of FDA-approved agents that target neural signalling pathways. As the field of cancer neuroscience continues to advance, the nervous system is increasingly recognized not only as a regulator of tumour behaviour but also as a promising target for therapeutic intervention.

摘要：The prevalence and incidence of unprovoked seizures and epilepsy markedly increase in older populations, most commonly secondary to cerebrovascular disease or neurodegenerative processes such as Alzheimer disease. This late-onset epilepsy (LOE) carries unique clinical challenges, including altered drug metabolism and drug efficacy. Despite this, animal models of aging are rarely included in the preclinical development of novel antiseizure medications (ASMs). Here, we provide a comprehensive perspective on the basic and cellular physiology and pharmacology underlying LOE pathogenesis and treatment. Aged rodents exhibit profoundly altered seizure susceptibility, including an increased vulnerability to focal seizures and epileptogenesis caused by physiological changes in the aging brain. Despite this, both aged rodents and older patients demonstrate an increased sensitivity to the anticonvulsant effects of several established ASMs. We highlight how this enhanced efficacy is driven not only by pharmacokinetic alterations but also by complex pharmacodynamic shifts, including altered GABAA and glutamate receptor subunit composition, ion channel modifications, and compensatory network adaptations. These occur on the background of neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and the accumulation of senescent cells, all emerging therapeutic targets in the epilepsy. Finally, this review explores the intersection of Alzheimer disease and epilepsy, and evaluates the potential of newer experimental therapeutic pathways, including mechanistic target of rapamycin inhibition, senolytics, sirtuin activators, and blood-brain barrier protection. By integrating basic cellular mechanisms with preclinical in vivo data, this review emphasizes the urgent need to incorporate aged animal models in ASM discovery pipelines to successfully identify novel therapies for the treatment of LOE and tailor treatments for the aging brain. SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT: Late-onset epilepsy (LOE) is the third most common neurological disorder in older people after stroke and dementia and is often associated with high cognitive, psychological, and psychosocial comorbidity burden and frailty, however, has received comparatively little attention. This review evaluates animal models for LOE, assesses the neurobiological mechanisms of the aged brain's increased sensitivity to seizures and epilepsy, and discusses current and potential novel therapeutic targets, providing a vital resource for advancing LOE research and improving therapeutic approaches.
