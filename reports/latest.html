摘要：Cyclin-dependent kinase-like 5 (CDKL5) is a serine/threonine kinase essential for brain development and function. Mutations in CDKL5 cause CDKL5 deficiency disorder (CDD), a severe early-onset epileptic encephalopathy associated with impaired synapse formation and function. However, how CDKL5 mutations disrupt synaptic function remains unclear. Here, we show that the major neuronal isoform of CDKL5 contains a C-terminal PDZ domain-binding motif that mediates direct binding to PSD-95 and SHANK PDZ domains and recruits CDKL5 to the postsynaptic density. Disrupting this motif mislocalized CDKL5, reduced dendritic spine density, and impaired spine maturation. We also show that the CDKL5 kinase domain binds the R8 rod domain of the mechanosensitive scaffold talin. Mutation of the talin-binding interface or reduced cellular contractility altered CDKL5 positioning relative to focal adhesions. These findings define how CDKL5 is targeted to synapses, suggest that talin mechanoswitching may position CDKL5 kinase activity, and highlight the importance of C-terminal anchoring for interpreting pathogenic truncations.

摘要：OBJECTIVE: Focal temporal lobe seizures often lead to impaired consciousness. However, the role of other key subcortical arousal systems such as dopamine is largely unknown. We aimed to address this gap by investigating dopamine dynamics in a mouse model of focal limbic seizures with impaired behavioral responsiveness. METHODS: We induced focal limbic seizures by brief electrical hippocampal stimulation in awake head-fixed mice with hippocampal and cortical local field potential recordings. Behavior was assessed by a conditioned auditory response task. We measured cortical dopamine with a genetically encoded fluorescent sensor via fiber photometry. Activity of dopaminergic neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) was recorded by calcium measurements. RESULTS: Focal hippocampal seizures were associated with altered cortical electrophysiological activity and impaired behavioral responsiveness. The sustained increases in dopamine suggest that the altered cortical function and impaired consciousness in focal limbic seizures may more closely resemble encephalopathy or delirium than deep sleep. Depressed transient dopamine release with sensory stimuli could also contribute to impaired behavioral responsiveness during seizures. Further work on the role of dopamine could have important therapeutic implications for altered cognition and behavior in people with epilepsy.

摘要：Epilepsia partialis continua (EPC) is a rare and challenging form of focal motor status epilepticus. Its occurrence strictly in systemic autoimmune disorders without overt encephalitis is exceptional. Corticosteroids are first-line therapy but may be limited by significant side effects and poor metabolic tolerability. We report a 54-year-old woman with Autoimmune Polyendocrine Syndrome (APS), including autoimmune hepatitis, latent autoimmune diabetes in adults, celiac disease, and hypothyroidism, presenting with continuous jerks of right III-V fingers. Neurological examination showed continuous, irregular jerks confined to the right III, IV, and V fingers. Extensive serum and CSF screenings, including neuronal antibodies, and MRI were unremarkable. Ictal HMPAO-SPECT revealed a focal hyperperfusion in the left hand-knob area of the primary motor cortex. Brain FDG-PET revealed focal hypometabolism in the same region. Video-EEG-EMG polygraphy showed intermittent spikes and polyspikes over the left central region preceding EMG bursts of the right V finger's abductor muscle with a latency of approximately 30-40 ms. These findings were consistent with EPC. Corticosteroids induced marked improvement but were discontinued due to severe glycemic worsening. Subsequent treatment with oral steroids, IVIG, and multiple antiseizure medications were ineffective. Administration of Rituximab led to complete and sustained clinical and electrophysiological remission, which persisted at the 18-month follow-up. 摘要：BACKGROUND: Quantifying skin sympathetic nerve activity (SKNA) is challenging because sympathetic signals are noisy, burst-like, and processing-dependent. Electrocardiogram-derived skin sympathetic nerve activity (ECG-derived SKNA) provides a non-invasive surrogate of sympathetic activity. Many existing SKNA analysis methods rely on semi-manual processing or static measurements, with limited attention to dynamic event-based physiology, reproducibility across large cohorts, or near-real-time applications. OBJECTIVE: To develop and evaluate a reproducible event-driven computational framework for analysing ECG-derived SKNA during transient sympathetic activation driven by simulated apnoeas, with potential application to future closed-loop neuromodulation. METHODS: The framework integrates electrocardiography preprocessing, multi-channel SKNA extraction, adaptive burst detection, event-centred physiological analysis, and sequential replay. It was developed using recordings from 50 pigs during apnoeas induced by intermittent negative upper airway pressure (INAP). Adaptive thresholds were defined as the maximum of a two-component Gaussian mixture model intersection, the baseline 95th percentile, and a baseline variability criterion. Sequential offline replay was performed on the pre-processed SKNA, with each replay decision restricted to SKNA samples available up to that decision point. Burst occupancy was calculated in a 30-s trailing window updated every 10 s, with trigger activation requiring occupancy above 5% in two consecutive windows. The pipeline was also applied to an independent multi-event INAP recording. RESULTS: During sequential replay, a trigger was present during INAP in 43 of 50 recordings (86%). CONCLUSION: This study presents a reproducible event-driven framework for analysing SKNA. The framework captures heterogeneous SKNA responses and allows sequential evaluation as new SKNA samples become available. By enabling dynamic SKNA responses to be monitored during physiological perturbations, the framework provides a practical foundation for prospective real-time validation and future closed-loop neuromodulation using SKNA-derived physiological feedback.

摘要：Metabolic disorders such as diabetes mellitus are global health crises driven by metabolic imbalance, chronic inflammation and dysfunction of the neuroimmune axis1-3. Electroceuticals targeting the autonomic nervous system have shown anti-inflammatory effects, yet existing systems lack the metabolic interoceptive feedback to fully restore immune-metabolic homeostasis4-9. Here we report an artificial neuroimmune axis (NeuroAxis) as a closed-loop immunomodulatory therapy for treating metabolic diseases. Analogous to its biological counterpart, NeuroAxis is solely powered by endogenous glucose, operating without batteries or wireless power transfer. It emulates the vagal reflex through closed-loop neuromodulation with metabolic interoceptive feedback. In a rat model of type 1 diabetes, NeuroAxis promotes the recovery of insulin-producing pancreatic β-cells and reduces inflammatory immune responses; in a rat model of type 2 diabetes, it drives macrophage remodelling and reduces systemic chronic inflammation. In both models, NeuroAxis restores glucose tolerance, improves insulin sensitivity and normalizes glycaemic control, with therapeutic effects persisting after treatment cessation. Our artificial neuroimmune axis promotes substantial advances in treating metabolic disorders and represents a general approach for autonomously regulating interorgan communication, central to homeostasis and pathophysiology.
