摘要：Hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) channels are essential for rhythmic electrical activity in cardiac and neuronal cells, and their dysfunction underlies disorders such as arrhythmia and epilepsy. Although HCN channels share a conserved six-transmembrane architecture with voltage-gated K⁺ (Kv) channels, how voltage-sensing domains (VSDs) are coupled to pore opening remains incompletely understood. Here, we identify an extracellular pathway that couples voltage-sensor movement to pore opening via the extracellular turret helix, which acts as a critical determinant of voltage-dependent gating in HCN channels. Alanine-scanning mutagenesis of HCN4 revealed six turret residues whose substitution abolished hyperpolarization-evoked currents. Voltage-clamp fluorometry showed that these mutations preserved S4 activation while selectively disrupting turret conformational changes, thereby uncoupling VSD movement from pore opening. Structural and functional analyses further identified an extracellular interaction network linking the turret to the S1, S5, and S6 segments that is essential for hyperpolarization-dependent gating. Collectively, these findings identify the extracellular turret as an active gating hub that coordinates concerted extracellular rearrangements to transmit voltage-sensor displacement to the pore. On the basis of these results, we propose a hand-in-hand coupling model, in which turret rearrangements act as a key downstream relay in the voltage-dependent opening of HCN channels.

摘要：We aimed to assess the frequency, clinical relevance, and prognostic value of stimulation-induced seizures (SIS) during intracranial EEG recording (iEEG), and to compare high-frequency (HF) and low-frequency (LF) stimulation protocols. Differences between HF and LF stimulation were assessed using odds ratios. Study-level meta-regression was conducted to evaluate associations between SIS rates, habitual seizure proportions, and postoperative favorable seizure outcome (Engel Ia). Instead, the clinical relevance of SIS depends on their similarity to habitual seizures. These findings suggest that SIS may reflect underlying network excitability, and that their diagnostic value lies in their semiological and EEG concordance rather than occurrence of stimulated seizures alone. 摘要：Refractory and superrefractory status epilepticus are usually treated with continuous intravenous anesthetic drugs to achieve seizure suppression, often for days or weeks. This strategy is supported by a compelling biological rationale; prolonged seizures can promote excitotoxicity, neuroinflammation, metabolic dysfunction, and neuronal injury. Yet direct evidence that deeper or longer anesthetic coma improves long-term neurological outcome remains limited. A recurrent clinical problem is that patients who receive the most aggressive treatment are also those with the longest, most severe, and most treatment-resistant forms of status epilepticus, often in the context of high-risk underlying etiologies. Clinical complexity, seizure activity, and treatment exposure therefore become tightly intertwined. This article argues that neurological outcome after refractory and superrefractory status epilepticus should be interpreted through the interaction of three domains: the underlying disease, the epileptic process, and the burden imposed by treatment. This perspective may help explain why survivors with different etiologies, including cryptogenic new onset refractory status epilepticus, febrile infection-related epilepsy syndrome, autoimmune encephalitis, developmental and epileptic encephalopathies, metabolic disorders, and acute symptomatic conditions, may converge toward a shared long-term phenotype characterized by cognitive impairment, functional disability, persistent epilepsy, and cerebral atrophy. Current evidence does not allow the relative contribution of these domains to be quantified in individual patients. It does, however, challenge a purely seizure-centered interpretation of outcome and supports a therapeutic aim of timely seizure control with the lowest cumulative anesthetic exposure required to achieve and maintain it. Future studies should model disease, seizure, and treatment burden together using longitudinal and time-dependent variables.

摘要：Status epilepticus (SE) is a neurological emergency requiring rapid behavioural control of convulsive activity. First-line benzodiazepines are frequently administered through delayed or invasive routes, and lose effectiveness as seizure persist. This study evaluated an intranasal embelin-β-cyclodextrin nanostructured lipid carrier (EBCD) as a behavioural proof-of-concept treatment in the lithium-pilocarpine rat model, using intranasal diazepam as reference. Treatment was withheld until continuous Racine stage 3-5 activity had been confirmed behaviourally. Seizures were scored for 60 min using modified Racine scale, and whole-tissue hippocampal GABA, glutamate, and acetylcholine quantified by LC-QTOF-MS; acetylcholine in the treated groups fell below the lowest calibrator and untreated-control glutamate exceeded the highest; both are semi-quantitative. The study was not powered as a non-inferiority comparison and does not establish equivalence. Hippocampal glutamate was elevated in untreated SE and reduced by both treatments (P = 0.014), indicating reduced glutamatergic tone, and GABA did not differ. METHODS: We reviewed all children (<19 years at surgery) who underwent unilobar extratemporal resection for DRE at Helsinki University Hospital, Finland's national center for pediatric epilepsy surgery, between 2003 and 2024. Because neural signatures are abstract, they require meaningful correlations with behavior, symptoms, and clinical outcomes. Ethical challenges arise when brain data, behavioral observations, and patient self-reports conflict, raising questions about whose knowledge matters and how to determine clinical "ground truth." We argue that neural signature research needs epistemic duty-the responsibility to make well-reasoned decisions using the best available evidence-and propose three strategies to address these challenges: contextual, dimensional approaches to integrate diverse data; involving patients in decisions about data use; and stress-testing neural models to assess their robustness under uncertainty and conflicting evidence.

摘要：CONTEXT: Painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy (PDPN) is a complex, progressive complication characterized by small-fiber degeneration, microvascular insufficiency, metabolic stress, and maladaptive central sensitization. First-line medications are generally only partially effective and frequently provide incomplete or insufficiently durable relief, underscoring the importance of mechanism-based interventions. EVIDENCE ACQUISITION: This narrative review was based on searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library from database inception through 2026, with priority given to English-language studies involving human subjects. The evidence was narratively synthesized and appraised by experts rather than systematically pooled, and no meta-analysis was performed. RESULTS: Neuromodulation directly modulates dysfunctional nociceptive circuits through approaches such as peripheral nerve stimulation (PNS), dorsal root ganglion stimulation (DRGS), and spinal cord stimulation (SCS). These emerging treatments may also provide microvascular and neuroimmune benefits. High-frequency and burst SCS provide nonparesthetic analgesia through mechanisms more congruent with endogenous pain-modulatory physiology, whereas closed-loop SCS based on evoked compound action potentials (ECAPs) enables real-time, stable engagement of neural tissue. Recent improvements in wireless platforms, miniaturized micro-electrode systems, and regenerative stimulation have increased therapeutic applications and decreased procedural burden while improving neural selectivity. Neuromodulation is progressing toward personalized, adaptive treatment through data-driven optimization of stimulation parameters and phenotype-specific programming, facilitated by artificial intelligence (AI). Biomarker-guided patient selection using quantitative sensory testing, intra-epidermal nerve fiber density, electrophysiologic measures, and advanced imaging is proposed as a framework for aligning therapeutic modality with disease phenotype. However, no biomarker has yet been validated to predict response to neuromodulation in PDPN, and this stratification remains hypothesis-generating and has not been prospectively validated. CONCLUSIONS: Neuromodulation is emerging as a mechanism-targeted and increasingly individualized approach for refractory PDPN that can provide sustained pain relief and functional benefits when other treatments have been ineffective. Preliminary evidence also suggests possible disease-modifying properties, although these require confirmation in PDPN-specific, long-term comparative trials.
